# Lightweight sleep animation — 0.9.16

Ditto settles into the supported sleeping pose for approximately 1.9 seconds, then the glossy body freezes. Only the three Zs and a cartoon mouth droplet continue moving. This is the approved lightweight approach; it does not continuously run the 3D renderer or jelly physics.

## Appearance and cadence

The droplet is one continuous translucent teardrop, replacing the overlapping round segments that looked like beads in 0.9.6. An analytic radius profile makes a long pointed upper tail and one rounded end. Its mouth attachment stays fixed; the tip moves by approximately 2 native pixels and sways by less than a pixel on the 5.2-second cycle shared with the first Z. The body's underlying shaded pixels remain visible through a blue tint, with a broad reflection and faint rim. There is no hard contour or necklace of highlights. This is a lightweight procedural overlay, not a refractive fluid simulation. No audio snore is played.

Ditto's purple body is now composed at 96% opacity against the black background, before RGB565 quantization. Against black this is a very subtle reduction in intensity; it does not simulate internal refraction or reveal an unseen environment. The glossy lighting, face, native resolution and physics are otherwise unchanged. The opacity is built into the shading palette once at startup and adds no runtime pixel blend.

The sleep compositor requests a nominal 12 Hz; scheduling produces about 11 updates/s on the measured board. Awake animation and minute clock/date updates use their existing paths. The panel's physical refresh rate is unchanged. Dim-to-10% and fast wake curves remain in [POWER-AND-SLEEP.md](POWER-AND-SLEEP.md).

## Work avoided

The final clean sleeping image is cached in PSRAM. Each overlay update restores and draws only two native pixel rectangles: 76 × 104 for the Zs and 40 × 56 for the droplet. Restoring the background prevents alpha accumulation or trails. The TE-synchronized presenter keeps both immutable framebuffers coherent and transfers only changed patches. The clock/header is refreshed when the minute changes, including midnight. Physics, skinning, rasterizer workers and audio remain parked between necessary changes.

Status separates `sleep_overlay_frames` from 3D `frames`; `render_parked` remains true when only sleep marks move. The compositor releases the continuous 240 MHz avatar lock. The RTOS can still boost the CPU while tasks/interrupts run, so this is not deep sleep or continuous 80 MHz operation.

## Evidence and limits

`tests/sleep_overlay_check.cpp` tests a full minute of animation, clipping and exact full-frame/tile/rectangle equivalence. `tests/partial_transport_check.py` exercises the production presenter with deferred DMA: 40 full frames, 400 small patches and 20 minute updates matched every physical pixel, including transitions between full and partial updates.

The final 0.9.4 nose-bubble test averaged 6.69% combined CPU busy during a 12-second sleep interval; the initial 0.9.5 ribbon test averaged 7.53%. A previous fully frozen 0.9.3 interval averaged 0.58%, while continuously animating the body at 10% brightness averaged 88.90%. These are short software-work samples across related firmware versions, not controlled current measurements. The 0.9.5 repeat passed stationary-body/audio counters, minute/midnight refresh, injected touch/motion wake, audio resume and transport checks. Its first interval was interrupted by a motion wake and is preserved as a failed sleep measurement, not averaged into the result.

The optimized 0.9.6 cartoon-droplet interval averaged **8.66% combined CPU busy** at **11.07 overlay updates/s**. The first shaded pass was 10.78% and is preserved separately; precomputing segment reciprocals and normalizing once per covered pixel reduced that work. Final checks are recorded in `test-results/lightweight-sleep-0.9.6/hardware-check.json`. Captures are exact digital framebuffer pixels; optical dimness and physical panel tearing cannot be inferred from them. Injected motion/touch tests do not establish real fingertip latency or GPIO38 timing. Existing quiet touch I²C deferrals remain counted and require physical qualification.

The 0.9.8 continuous translucent droplet passed the same hardware checks: **6.80% combined CPU busy** during a 12-second quiet interval, **11.57 overlay updates/s**, parked body/physics/audio counters, correct minute/midnight refresh, injected touch/motion wakes and audio resume. Captured motion changed 813 pixels, all in the intended clock/overlay bands. The 60-second follow-up recorded no display/audio/TE failures, resets or touch queue overflows. Quiet I²C deferrals remain; physical digitizer qualification is still open. One earlier interval ended after a motion wake and is preserved under `motion-interrupted/`, excluded from the quiet-work figure. Enlarged 600% framebuffer comparisons were inspected for the former bead contour.

The 0.9.9 performance build preserves the 0.9.8 appearance. The capture comparison changed 799 pixels entirely inside the expected bands. The 60-second follow-up had no display/audio/TE errors, reset or queue overflow; 116 quiet touch I²C errors remained counted with zero controller recoveries. See `test-results/lightweight-sleep-0.9.9/hardware-check.json` and the copy in the 0.9.9 release evidence. Physical touch qualification remains open.
